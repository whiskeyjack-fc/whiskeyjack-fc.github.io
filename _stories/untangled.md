The sheets are soft, wrapped around me. Warm where they touch my skin, cool where they've pulled away. Comforting in a way I haven't felt in days.

I blink at the ceiling. White, textured with those tiny bumps from layers of old paint. Still unfamiliar. Still not quite mine, even after a week of staring at it every morning. Yesterday feels distant, soft around the edges. But warm. Comforting. Something happened. Something good? I'm not quite awake enough to remember.

My ankle throbs. Right. The jog. The stupid jog I thought would help clear my head after five days straight of orientations and class schedules and pretending I knew where the hell I was going. Instead my foot caught and twisted on some uneven pavement two blocks from the residence, hobbled back like an idiot, trying not to cry from the pain or the embarrassment or the sudden crash of everything.

The first week here had been pure survival mode. Wake up, figure out which building, which room, which professor. Smile at people in hallways. Nod like I understood the campus map. Keep moving. Don't stop. Don't think about home or Mom's cooking or my old bedroom where I actually knew where the light switch was without fumbling.

Yesterday was supposed to be different. A day off. Sleep in, explore the neighborhood, maybe find a coffee shop that could become *my* coffee shop. Instead I came through the door limping, probably looking as pathetic as I felt.

And there was Kai.

I'd seen them before. Worn-in running shoes, headphones perpetually on, usually carrying a medical textbook or gym bag, but we'd only exchanged the kind of nods you give people you share a kitchen with but don't actually know. Yesterday though, they were in the common area, and the second they saw me hopping on one foot they were up.

"Whoa, hey—what happened?"

Before I could answer they'd pulled out a chair, guided me into it, disappeared and returned with an ice pack wrapped in a dish towel.

"Keep it elevated." They grabbed another chair, positioned it so I could prop my foot up. Their hands were sure, confident. "How long ago?"

"Maybe... twenty minutes?"

"Okay. Not too bad. Keep the ice on for fifteen, then fifteen off. You got any compression bandages?"

I shook my head.

"I'll grab one from my room. I've got extras." They paused, studying my face. "You okay? You look..."

And that's when it happened. The wall I'd been building all week just... crumbled. My eyes burned. My throat tightened. I tried to swallow it down but my body had other plans.

"Hey." Kai's voice softened. They crouched next to the chair. "It's the adrenaline crash. Happens after injuries. Your body's been running on stress hormones and now they're dropping. It's totally normal."

I nodded, not trusting my voice, tears spilling over anyway.

"Plus you're probably dehydrated and tired. When did you last eat?"

I couldn't remember.

"Right. Okay. Physical stuff first, then you'll feel better. Promise."

They were so calm about it. Like my complete breakdown was just another symptom to treat. They got me water, made me drink it, adjusted the ice pack. Kept talking in this steady, practical way about inflammation and endorphins and how the body processes stress.

It helped. Kind of. Enough that I could breathe again.

That's when Sasha appeared in the doorway. I'd seen them before, of course. Thoughtful eyes, gentle smile, usually with a mug of something warm. We'd exchanged a few words in passing but never really talked. Something about them always seemed... safe. Steady.

"What's going on?" They took in the scene—me with my red eyes and elevated ankle, Kai with their ice pack and lecture posture—and immediately came over. Where Kai moved with efficiency, Sasha moved with this gentle deliberateness, like they were approaching something fragile.

Kai filled them in quickly. "Rolled ankle. Adrenaline crash. Possibly low blood sugar."

Sasha nodded slowly, but their eyes stayed on my face. They pulled up a chair, sat down. Didn't touch me but somehow their presence felt like a hand on my shoulder.

"That sounds really overwhelming," they said quietly. "The injury, I mean. But also... everything else?"

I stared at them. How did they—

"I'm guessing this is your first time living away from home?" Their smile was small, kind. "It's a lot. Even without the ankle."

And just like that, I was crying again. But different this time. Not the panicky, can't-breathe crying. Just... release.

"I miss my mom," I heard myself say. "And I don't know anyone here and I've been so scared to talk to people and I thought if I just kept busy I wouldn't feel so—"

"Alone?" Sasha offered.

I nodded.

"That makes sense. New place, new people, pressure to have everything figured out. Your brain's been in survival mode. Fight or flight. And you can't sustain that." They glanced at Kai. "The physical stress probably amplified the emotional stuff."

"And the emotional stress definitely didn't help the physical coordination," Kai added, almost smiling. "Stress affects proprioception. Spatial awareness. Makes you more prone to injury."

I laughed through my tears. "So you're both saying I did this to myself?"

"No," they said together.

"We're saying it's all connected," Sasha continued. "Mind, body, circumstance. You can't separate them."

Kai nodded. "Fix one, you help the others."

I looked between them. Kai, hands steady as they finished wrapping my ankle. Sasha, eyes warm and patient. These people I'd been too anxious to introduce myself to. Who'd been here the whole time.

"I'm sorry I've been so..." I gestured vaguely at myself.

"Human?" Sasha suggested.

"Adjusting?" Kai offered.

"Weird and antisocial?"

"You weren't weird," Kai said. "Little intense maybe. But everyone's intense first week."

"You smiled at me on Tuesday," Sasha added. "In the kitchen. That wasn't antisocial."

I barely remembered that. But they did.

Something loosened in my chest. Not fixed, but... less tight.

"Thank you," I said. "Both of you. This is... I really needed this."

"You need food too," Kai said, standing. "I'm making pasta. You're eating pasta."

"I'll make tea," Sasha said. "Unless you want coffee?"

"Tea's good."

They moved around the kitchen and I sat there, ice pack numbing my ankle, watching them work. They debated the garlic situation. Kai's position: definitely adding it. Sasha's position: maybe show some restraint. My position: more garlic, always more garlic. They asked me about my major, where I was from, what kind of music I liked. Normal questions but they felt huge. Like laying down foundation.

We ate at the kitchen table. Then migrated to the couch. The conversation kept flowing, easy and endless. When I finally noticed the windows, it was dark outside and we were still talking about everything and nothing. Kai told stories about their worst sports injuries (the ankle thing suddenly felt very minor). Sasha admitted they'd almost dropped out their first semester because they were so homesick (I wasn't alone). I told them about hand-drawing a custom tarot deck with five suits instead of four on my tablet (made it halfway through before school started up again and they're still sitting unfinished in a folder somewhere).

Kai leaned forward when I mentioned the tarot deck. "Wait, five suits? Traditional tarot only has four. What's the fifth one?"

I pulled out my tablet, opened the drawing app and found the files. Their shoulder pressed against mine as they looked at the screen. Warm. Solid.

"These are really good," they said quietly. "You should finish them."

"Maybe." I was very aware of how close they were sitting. "If I ever have time again."

"Make time." They looked up at me, and there was something in their eyes I couldn't quite read. Soft. Intent. "For things that matter."

Sasha shifted closer on my other side, joining us in looking at the sketches. "I love this one. The way you've drawn the figures." Their fingers traced the screen, just barely brushing mine. They didn't pull away. Neither did I.

"You have really good instincts," Sasha continued. "About symbolism. About meaning." They paused, looking at me then, really looking at me. "You understand people, even if talking to them terrifies you." I felt seen in a way that made my chest tight. Like they'd named a truth I hadn't admitted to myself.

At some point I realize I'm breathing easy. Deep, natural breaths I haven't managed all week.

Kai's hands move while they talk, expressive and sure, and I can't stop watching. My heart does something complicated. They catch me staring once, and instead of looking away they smile. Small, knowing. Not mocking. Curious maybe. Interested.

And Sasha. When they laugh, they touch my arm, my knee, my shoulder. Light touches that linger just a moment longer than casual. And I find myself hoping they'll do it again. Leaning in slightly. Making it easier.

I catch them exchanging glances with each other. Quick, meaningful. The kind of look that only happens between people who've known each other a long time. There's something complicated in those glances. Recognition maybe. Understanding. Like they're both realizing the same thing at the same time and checking in about it. But there's no tension. No edge. Just this quiet acknowledgment passing between them, and then they're both looking back at me. Both smiling.

It doesn't make me feel excluded. If anything, it makes me feel seen. Like whatever silent conversation just happened, I was the subject of it. And it was good.

"You're different than I thought," Kai says eventually. "I mean. I saw you around and thought you seemed interesting. But I didn't know if you'd want to talk to us."

"I thought the same about both of you," I admit. "You both seemed so... put together. Like you had it figured out."

Sasha laughs softly. "We absolutely don't."

"But maybe we could figure it out together?" Kai's voice is casual but their eyes are anything but. There's a question there. An offer.

"I'd like that," I say. And mean it more than I've meant anything in weeks.

The energy in the room has shifted. Warmer somehow. Charged. Not uncomfortable. Just aware. Like we're all standing at the edge of something new and deciding whether to step forward.

A few hours ago I was ready to give up. And now... this.

"I should probably sleep," I said finally, reluctantly. It was past midnight. "Class tomorrow."

"How's the ankle?" Kai asked.

I tested it gingerly. "Better. Still sore but better."

"Keep it wrapped tomorrow. Keep icing it through the day."

"You know where to find us," Sasha added. "And maybe don't try to white-knuckle your way through homesickness alone next time?"

"Noted."

We said our goodnights. Reluctant, drawn out, nobody quite wanting to leave. Kai's hand on my shoulder, warm and steadying. Sasha's hug, brief but solid. I went to my room feeling lighter than I had in weeks. Maybe ever.

I must have fallen asleep fast because I don't remember my head hitting the pillow.

I do remember, distantly, a soft knock. My door opening. Sheets rustling.

"Is this okay?" A whisper in the dark.

"Yeah," I whispered back. Not sure who. Too tired to care. Just grateful.

Now, morning light filtering through the blinds, I'm very awake. Very aware.

Someone is behind me. I can feel their warmth, their breathing. The weight of an arm draped over my waist.

Was it Kai? Their confidence, their steadiness, the way they fixed things. The way they'd known exactly what to do when I was falling apart.

Or Sasha? Their gentleness, their insight, the way they'd seen past the surface. The way they'd made me feel understood.

I'd been drawn to both of them. Equally. Differently but equally. Is that even possible? Last night felt like opening a door I didn't know was there. Or maybe two doors.

I should turn around. Look. Know.

But if I look, it becomes real. Specific. One or the other. And right now, in this tangled moment, it's both. It's possibility. It's the feeling of someone coming back, choosing to stay, taking that chance.

They explained so much yesterday. The physical breakdown. My body giving out, the crash after running on stress. The emotional overwhelm. The homesickness, the fear, the loneliness. The combination of circumstances that led to all of it. They got everything right, it was all true.

But they couldn't predict this fourth truth. What I'm feeling right now, heart pounding, afraid to move.

Hope.

Not the generic kind. The specific, terrifying, wonderful kind. The kind that comes from connecting with people. From being seen. From the possibility (however it unfolds) that I'm not doing this alone anymore. Not the semester, not the homesickness, not the whole messy process of becoming whoever I'm supposed to be.

The person behind me shifts slightly. Mumbles something in their sleep.

I close my eyes. Breathe. Let myself feel it all. The anxiety, yes, still there. But also the excitement. The anticipation. The sense that yesterday broke something open and today I get to find out what comes next.

Eventually I'll turn around. Eventually I'll know.

But for now, for this moment, I let myself be here. In this uncertainty. In this tangle of sheets and possibility and new beginnings.

In this perfect, terrifying, hopeful unknown.
