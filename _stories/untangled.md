The sheets are soft, wrapped around me. Warm where they touch my skin, cool where they've pulled away. Comforting in a way I haven't felt in days.

I blink at the ceiling — white, textured with those tiny bumps from layers of old paint, still not quite mine even after a week of staring at it every morning. Yesterday feels distant and soft around the edges. Whatever happened yesterday, my chest remembers it was good. My head just hasn't caught up.

My ankle throbs. Right. The jog. The stupid jog I thought would help clear my head after five days straight of orientations and class schedules and pretending I knew where the hell I was going. Instead my foot caught and twisted on some uneven pavement two blocks from the residence, hobbled back like an idiot, trying not to cry from the pain or the embarrassment or the sudden crash of everything.

The first week here had been pure survival mode. Wake up, figure out which building, which room, which professor. Smile at people in hallways. Nod along in lectures, having read way more than I needed to. Keep moving. Don't stop. Don't think about home or Mom's cooking or my old bedroom where I actually knew where the light switch was without fumbling.

Yesterday was supposed to be different. A day off. Sleep in, explore the neighborhood, maybe find a coffee shop that could become *my* coffee shop. Instead I came through the door limping, probably looking as pathetic as I felt.

And there was Kai.

I'd seen them before. Worn-in running shoes, headphones perpetually on, usually carrying a medical textbook or gym bag, but we'd only nodded at each other in the kitchen a few times, never actually talked. Yesterday though, they were in the common area, and the second they saw me hopping on one foot they were up.

"Whoa, hey—what happened?"

Before I could answer they'd pulled out a chair, guided me into it, disappeared and returned with an ice pack wrapped in a dish towel.

"Keep it elevated." They grabbed another chair, positioned it so I could prop my foot up. Their hands were confident. "How long ago?"

"Maybe... twenty minutes?"

Kai's thumb swiped over their phone screen, quick, before they answered. "Okay. Not too bad. Keep the ice on for fifteen, then fifteen off. You got any compression bandages?"

I shook my head.

"I'll grab one from my room. I've got extras." They paused, studying my face. "You okay? You look..."

And that's when it happened. The wall I'd been building all week just... crumbled. My eyes burned. My throat tightened. I tried to swallow it down but my body had other plans.

"Hey." Kai's voice softened. They crouched next to the chair. "It's just an adrenaline crash. You held it together until you didn't have to anymore, that's all." They hesitated, just slightly, before adding, "That's the theory, anyway. Feels different when it's an actual person, not a textbook case."

I nodded, not trusting my voice, tears spilling over anyway.

"Plus you're probably dehydrated and tired. When did you last eat?"

I couldn't remember.

"Right. Okay. Physical stuff first, then you'll feel better. Promise."

They were so calm about it, treating my complete breakdown as just another symptom. They got me water, made me drink it, adjusted the ice pack. Kept talking, low and steady, until my breathing evened out.

That's when Sasha appeared in the doorway. I'd seen them before, of course. Thoughtful eyes, gentle smile, usually with a mug cradled in both hands. We'd exchanged a few words in passing but never really talked. They always seemed to take their time with things. Never in a hurry.

"What's going on?" They took in the scene: my red eyes, the elevated ankle, Kai's ice pack and lecture posture, and came straight over. Where Kai moved with efficiency, Sasha eased into the room, careful with every step.

Kai filled them in quickly. "Rolled ankle. Adrenaline crash. Possibly low blood sugar."

Sasha nodded slowly, but their eyes stayed on my face. They crossed the room first, tugged the blanket off the back of the couch, and settled it over my shoulders before pulling up a chair and sitting down.

"That sounds really overwhelming," they said quietly. "The injury, I mean. But also... everything else?"

I stared at them. How did they—

"I'm guessing this is your first time living away from home?" Their smile was small, kind. "It's a lot. Even without the ankle."

And just like that, I was crying again. Quieter this time. Just... release.

"I miss my mom," I heard myself say. "And I don't know anyone here and I've been so scared to talk to people and I thought if I just kept busy I wouldn't feel so—"

"Alone?" Sasha offered.

I nodded.

"That makes sense. New place, new people, and you're supposed to have it together on day one." They glanced at Kai. "I bet you've been running on empty since you got here, and today's just when it caught up."

"Yeah, and running on empty means you're not really watching where you put your feet," Kai added, almost smiling. "So. Twisted ankle, more or less inevitable."

I laughed through my tears. "So you're both saying I did this to myself?"

"No—" Sasha started.

"Kind of," Kai said, at the same time.

Sasha glanced at them. Kai didn't back down. "Distracted, sleep-deprived, on autopilot. I've read this exact case before. That's not an accusation. It's just true."

"It's not helpful," Sasha said, quieter.

"It's honest." Kai's tone didn't change.

Nobody spoke for a second. Sasha's jaw stayed tight, and I couldn't tell if they'd actually let it go, or just didn't want to keep arguing about it in front of me.

Sasha's tone shifted, like they were quoting somebody else. "Or so the theory goes." Then, steadier: "We're saying it's all connected. Mind, body, circumstance. You can't separate them."

Kai nodded. "Fix one, you help the others."

I looked between them. Kai, hands steady as they finished wrapping my ankle. Sasha, eyes patient, not moving from my face. These people I'd been too anxious to introduce myself to. Who'd been here the whole time.

"I'm sorry I've been so..." I gestured vaguely at myself.

"Human?" Sasha suggested.

"Adjusting?" Kai offered.

"Weird and antisocial?"

"You weren't weird," Kai said. "Little intense maybe. But everyone's intense first week."

"You smiled at me on Tuesday," Sasha added. "In the kitchen. That wasn't antisocial."

I barely remembered that. But they did.

That shouldn't have meant as much as it did.

"Thank you," I said. "Both of you. This is... I really needed this."

"You need food too," Kai said, standing. "I'm making pasta. You're eating pasta."

"I'll make tea," Sasha said. "Unless you want coffee?"

"Tea's good."

They moved around the kitchen and I sat there, ice pack numbing my ankle, watching them work. Kai eyeballed every measurement. Sasha checked each one before it went into the pot. They asked me about my major, where I was from, what kind of music I liked.

We ate at the kitchen table. Kai reached for the salt before tasting the pasta.

Sasha caught their wrist. "You do that every time."

"It needs it every time."

"You have not tasted it."

"I watched you make it."

"Watching isn't the same as trusting it."

I held out my hand. Kai passed me the salt without looking at Sasha.

"Don't encourage them," Sasha said.

I tasted the pasta, then sprinkled some over my plate. "It does need it."

Kai leaned back, pleased with themself. Then they caught me shifting in my chair. "Still tender?" Not smug this time. Just noticing.

"It's fine."

Kai let it go.

Sasha looked between the two of us. "I'm timing how long you two agree on anything." They reached for the salt too.

We moved to the couch after dinner. The conversation kept going, easy now. Kai told stories about their worst sports injuries (the ankle thing suddenly felt very minor). Sasha admitted they'd almost dropped out their first semester because they were so homesick (I wasn't alone).

Nobody spoke for a moment. I could have let it pass, same as always. Instead I pushed up out of the chair, ankle protesting, and came back a few minutes later with my tablet.

"I've been drawing a tarot deck," I said, before I could talk myself out of it. "Five suits instead of four. It's not finished. I don't really show people."

Kai went quiet, studying me instead of the tablet. My thumb found the edge of the screen, ready to close the file before they said anything.

Then Kai sat up straighter. "Five suits? What's the extra one?"

"I don't know yet." I opened the file anyway and turned the screen so they could both see. Kai's shoulder pressed warm against mine as they leaned in.

I tapped one card in the fifth suit and pointed it out. "What do you think this one means?"

Kai tapped the screen to zoom in and studied it before answering. "It's really good. I don't know tarot, though. I'd just be making something up."

"Make something up, then."

"...Something about not finishing what you start?" They said it slow, testing it, watching my face for whether they'd gotten it wrong.

It landed closer than I expected. Close enough that I didn't trust myself not to give it away.

I flicked to the next card before either of us had to say anything else, and Kai let it go.

Sasha leaned in from my other side, fingers just barely brushing mine as they reached for the tablet.

If Kai could get that close off one card, I wanted to know what Sasha would see in all of it.

"Sasha," I said. "Why do you think I never finished it?"

Sasha didn't rush it. When they spoke, it came out careful, considered. "Maybe you're saving it," they said finally, "for whenever you're sure enough of something to actually finish it."

That wasn't it. Sasha assumed it was something I'd eventually solve. I wasn't sure it would. They waited for me to say they'd gotten it right. I opened my mouth to give them the real answer instead, and didn't.

I was still watching Kai's hands when Sasha's hand found my arm and stayed.

I caught them glance at each other, quick, gone before I could guess what it meant. I didn't ask. Whatever it was, they both looked back at me a second later, still smiling.

Kai's shoulder against mine. Sasha's hand still on my arm. Any other night, I'd have pulled away by now. I hadn't.

"You're different than I thought," Kai said eventually. "I saw you around and thought you seemed interesting. Didn't know if you'd want to talk to us."

"I thought the same about both of you," I admitted. "I thought you both already had it figured out."

Sasha laughed softly. "We absolutely don't." A beat. "Last practicum I misread a client for two weeks. Confidently. To their face."

"I still had to look up basic ankle-taping last month," Kai admitted. "In front of an actual patient."

I opened my mouth once and nothing came out. The second time, I made myself keep going, throat tight.

"So what happens now?" My voice came out steadier than I felt. "With both of you. I'm not picking. I don't think I can, not without losing something either way."

Nobody filled the silence right away. Kai looked at Sasha. Sasha looked at the floor, then back at me.

"I don't actually know." Sasha looked at Kai, just for a second, then back at me. "There's a second question in this. Not just yours."

"Same." Kai glanced at Sasha, then away. "Hadn't allowed myself to think it."

It wasn't the answer I wanted. But nobody had smoothed it over to make me feel better, and that counted for something.

"Okay," I said. "Then we don't know yet."

We let it sit there a moment.

"I should probably sleep," I said. It was past midnight. "Class tomorrow."

"How's the ankle?" Kai asked.

I tested it gingerly. "Better. Still sore but better."

"Keep it wrapped tomorrow. Keep icing it through the day."

"You know where to find us," Sasha added. "And maybe don't try to white-knuckle your way through homesickness alone next time?"

"Noted."

We said our goodnights. Reluctant, drawn out, nobody quite wanting to leave. Kai's hand on my shoulder, firm. Sasha's hug, brief but solid. I went to my room feeling lighter than I had in weeks. Maybe ever, even without an answer.

I remember all of it now. I don't move to get up right away. Kai's shoulder against mine over the tablet. Sasha's fingers brushing mine, not pulling away. Two different weights, still sitting on my skin.

I'd been drawn to both of them. Not the same way, but just as much. Is that even possible? I'd asked them outright and gotten nothing certain back. Just two people admitting they didn't know either.

Both, then. Whatever that turns out to mean.

My ankle is stiff when I finally sit up, but it holds. Getting free of the sheets takes longer than it should.

I don't get all the way untangled. I get up anyway.
